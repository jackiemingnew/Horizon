## [AI Industry Open Letters Clash Over Open-Weight Model Policy](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/2/open-letters/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Microsoft-led open letter signed by 235 AI companies, including NVIDIA, Amazon, and OpenAI, argues against U.S. government restrictions on open-weight models. Around the same time, Anthropic declined to sign and published its own position, while the 'Pacing the Frontier' letter from 1,324 frontier AI employees urged the U.S. to support international efforts to pace automated AI development. This policy clash could directly shape U.S. AI regulation and determine whether open-weight models remain freely available or face restrictions based on safety concerns. The involvement of top AI companies and researchers signals a deep divide over how to balance open innovation, national security, and AI safety. The Microsoft-led letter explicitly defends distillation—training models on other models' outputs—as a legitimate development technique, warning policymakers not to conflate it with misappropriation. Dario Amodei, in Anthropic's response, called for a crackdown on industrial-scale distillation operations while insisting Anthropic has never advocated a ban on open-weights models.

**Background**: Open-weight models are AI models whose trained parameters (weights) are publicly released, allowing developers to inspect, fine-tune, and deploy them beyond what closed API-only models permit. Proponents argue this enables external scrutiny and broad innovation, while critics point to potential misuse for cyberattacks or biological attacks and the difficulty of preventing unauthorized distillation.

## [AI chip count to double every 9 months, hit 200 million by 2028](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/07/29/technology/ai-chips-data-center-boom.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Epoch AI estimates that the global inventory of AI chips is now about 20 million and is doubling every nine months, reaching roughly 200 million by the end of 2028. IDC projects that global AI infrastructure investment will exceed $1 trillion by 2029, up from $318 billion last year. This rapid growth shows that the AI boom is being driven by scaling laws, with compute size viewed as the main lever for capability. The enormous scale of investment raises critical questions about electricity demand, environmental impact, geopolitical balance, and whether current spending can ever be profitable. The United States controls roughly 80% of global AI compute, and Google alone is believed to hold about four times as many AI chips as all Chinese companies combined. Economists warn that infrastructure spending may exceed earnings, pointing to historical boom-and-bust cycles in large-scale infrastructure projects.

**Background**: Neural scaling laws are empirical patterns showing that model performance improves predictably as compute, parameters, and training data increase. Epoch AI is a nonprofit research institute that studies AI trajectory and governance by analyzing historical data on machine-learning compute and algorithmic efficiency. The New York Times article is an analysis that contextualizes these projections by discussing energy costs, geopolitical competition, and possible economic risks.
