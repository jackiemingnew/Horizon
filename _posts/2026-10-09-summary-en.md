## [China's Tsinghua team builds first stably operating nuclear optical clock](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11122-1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

A Tsinghua University research team has become the first in the world to build and stably operate a nuclear optical clock, using a self-developed 148 nm continuous-wave vacuum ultraviolet laser together with a thorium-229-doped calcium fluoride crystal, with the results published in Nature. The clock uses the energy-level transition of the thorium-229 nucleus as its timekeeping reference. Nuclear clocks are expected to be roughly an order of magnitude more precise than today's best atomic clocks, so a working, stable device could eventually support a redefinition of the SI second as well as more accurate satellite navigation, deep-space tracking and tests of fundamental physics. It also marks a shift in precision metrology from electron transitions to nuclear transitions, an area where China has now demonstrated a first. The clock references the thorium-229m isomer's exceptionally low-energy transition of about 8.36 eV, corresponding to a wavelength of 148.38 nm in the vacuum ultraviolet, a region where narrow-linewidth continuous-wave lasers are notoriously hard to build. This work follows closely on other 2026 demonstrations of continuous-wave 148 nm sources, including four-wave mixing in cadmium vapour and second-harmonic generation in strontium tetraborate, which together made resonant driving of the thorium nucleus practical.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 8, 05:19

**Background**: Conventional atomic clocks count oscillations of electrons moving between energy levels around a nucleus, whereas a nuclear clock instead uses a transition inside the atomic nucleus itself; because the nucleus is far less sensitive to stray electromagnetic fields and collisions, such a clock should be more stable and more precise. Thorium-229 is uniquely suited to this because its lowest excited state, the isomer thorium-229m, sits only about 8.36 eV above the ground state, low enough to be driven by a laser in the vacuum ultraviolet, unlike any other known nuclear isomer. Embedding thorium-229 in a crystal such as calcium fluoride allows many nuclei to be probed at once, which is the solid-state approach that made stable operation feasible.

## [OpenAI API Adds Ultrafast Mode for GPT-6.1 Sol](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog) ⭐️ 8.0/10

OpenAI has introduced an "Ultrafast" service tier for GPT-6.1 Sol in the Responses API (v1/responses), offering up to roughly 8x faster generation than the Standard tier. The tier is available to all API users and is priced at 6x Standard, quoted at about $12 per million input tokens, $0.60 for cached input, and $60 per million output tokens at short context. This gives latency-sensitive production workloads — real-time agents, interactive coding assistants, and multi-step workflows — a way to buy speed without switching to a different model. It also formalizes a premium speed tier in OpenAI's pricing, pushing the familiar cost-versus-latency trade-off directly into the API billing model for developers. The quoted prices apply to short-context usage, so longer prompts or outputs will cost more, and the 8x speedup is described as a maximum relative to Standard rather than a guaranteed throughput. The change appears as a changelog entry with no published independent benchmarks or detailed rate-limit and availability notes yet.

**Background**: GPT-6.1 Sol is part of OpenAI's GPT-6.1 model family, released on September 29, 2026 alongside Astra, and is positioned as a balance of capability and cost for everyday professional work such as coding, document understanding, and multi-step business tasks. The Responses API, introduced in March 2025, is OpenAI's interface for building agentic applications, supporting stateful interactions and built-in tools like file search, web search, and computer use. Service tiers let developers choose a price-performance point for the same model instead of changing models to adjust speed.

## [SpaceX to Acquire Nationwide Low-Band Spectrum for Starlink Mobile](https://x.com/SpaceX/status/2108291133025698301) ⭐️ 8.0/10

SpaceX announced an agreement to acquire a nationwide portfolio of low-band spectrum licenses — up to 14 MHz of paired spectrum in the 800 MHz band — from Grain Management for its Starlink Mobile service. The company said combining this spectrum with its Gen2 constellation will let Starlink Mobile deliver high-speed mobile broadband to Americans wherever they are. Low-band spectrum penetrates buildings and travels long distances, filling the single biggest technical gap that has kept satellite-to-phone service from competing with terrestrial carriers. If completed, the deal would turn Starlink from a satellite broadband provider into a direct nationwide mobile carrier rival to AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile — whose shares fell sharply on the news. The portfolio consists of up to 14 MHz of paired spectrum in the 800 MHz band, which SpaceX described as addressing "one of the key remaining technical gaps" and as enabling Starlink Mobile to work indoors. The transaction remains subject to regulatory approval, including from the FCC, and no financial terms were disclosed.

**Background**: Low-band spectrum generally refers to frequencies below 1 GHz, where signals propagate far and penetrate walls, making it the most prized resource in mobile networking — T-Mobile built a coverage advantage on 600 MHz spectrum for exactly this reason. Satellite direct-to-cell services have historically relied on line-of-sight links to satellites, so they struggle indoors, in tunnels and in dense urban canyons, which is the core reason Starlink needs terrestrial spectrum. Gen2 is SpaceX's second-generation Starlink constellation; the FCC has authorized an additional 7,500 Gen2 satellites, bringing the total authorized to roughly 15,000 spacecraft designed for higher capacity and mobile coverage from space.
