## [Terence Tao Unveils 'Math 2.0' Vision for AI-Era Mathematics](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/slides/math-2-0-caltech-2026.pdf) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Fields Medalist Terence Tao has published a Caltech slide deck dated October 2026 titled "Math 2.0," arguing that AI has ended the era in which proofs were scarce and that mathematics must now decenter raw problem solving. In the talk and an accompanying blog post, he sketches a historical "Math 1.0" state, the current disruption caused by AI systems solving open problems, and a future "Math 2.0" in which AI tools sustain both the mathematical community and the role of human mathematical understanding. Coming from one of the world's most influential living mathematicians, the vision carries weight in shaping how the mathematical community, journals, and funders respond to AI-driven discovery. It reframes the debate from "can AI do math?" to "what should mathematicians value once AI can solve problems," a question that affects hiring, grant evaluation, publishing, and how a generation of young researchers chooses problems. Among the deck's most debated points are three slides on medicine and cancer cures, where Tao questions whether a drug with no human-understandable mechanism should be administered or prescribed. He also uses the phrase "open problems are lighthouses, not destinations" to argue that unsolved problems are primarily valuable for revealing gaps in theory rather than as trophies to be captured.

**Background**: Terence Tao is a Fields Medalist at UCLA and one of the most widely followed mathematicians online, writing regularly about AI and mathematics on his blog. Recent AI systems have begun producing solutions or substantial partial progress on long-standing open problems, which Tao has described as a shift from a world where proofs were the scarce resource to one where they may become abundant. His "Math 1.0 / 2.0" framing distinguishes the traditional, human-paced practice of mathematics from a future practice reshaped by AI collaboration, and it echoes broader "AI for Science" efforts to integrate machine learning into scientific discovery.

## [Japan Declares Cyber Emergency After Surge in Attacks on Major Firms](https://www.ft.com/content/2d4333d1-8119-44a4-a5a5-2ee9ef2544fa?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Japanese government declared a "cyber space state of emergency" after more than 20 major companies were hit by attacks in recent weeks, affecting railways, automakers, hospitals and government facilities, with tens of millions of customer records potentially exposed. JR East said its ticket-booking system may have leaked the email addresses of 1.67 million customers, and several incidents have been linked to ransomware at SoftBank-affiliated IDC Frontier. A national-level emergency declaration signals that attacks have moved beyond isolated corporate breaches to threaten critical infrastructure such as rail, healthcare and finance, which could affect the daily lives of millions of Japanese residents. It also puts pressure on Japanese firms to overhaul long-criticized, weak cybersecurity defenses, and may set a precedent for other governments facing similar industrial-scale attack waves. Japan's Financial Services Agency has asked banks to stop accepting driver's licenses as identity proof for new account openings and instead require chip-bearing identity documents. Experts say Japanese defenses are weak and that AI is lowering the barrier to carrying out complex attacks, though there is currently no evidence linking the latest incidents to AI.

**Background**: Ransomware is a type of malware that encrypts or steals a victim's data and holds it hostage until a ransom is paid, often demanded in cryptocurrency to make tracing harder. Japan has historically ranked poorly on corporate cybersecurity readiness, and a chip-based identity card stores data on an embedded integrated circuit, making it harder to forge than a plain driver's license.
