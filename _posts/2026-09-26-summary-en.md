## [SemiAnalysis Publishes Free Teardown of Intel Panther Lake and 18A](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/intel-panther-lake-teardown) ⭐️ 9.0/10

SemiAnalysis published a free STEEL teardown examining Intel's Panther Lake client processor and the Intel 18A process node it is built on, offering a physical look inside Intel's most advanced in-house manufacturing technology. The piece is the first detailed public dissection of a shipping 18A-based product, following Intel's formal unveiling of Panther Lake as its first AI PC platform on 18A. Intel 18A is the linchpin of Intel's foundry ambitions and its effort to regain process leadership, so independent physical analysis of a real 18A chip is a rare, high-signal data point for the semiconductor industry. The findings could shape how potential foundry customers, competitors such as TSMC and Samsung, and hardware engineers judge Intel's ability to deliver on its roadmap. Panther Lake uses a disaggregated, multi-chiplet design: a heterogeneous CPU core tile on Intel 18A, an integrated graphics tile based on the Arc Xe3 architecture derived from Xe2 (Battlemage), and an I/O tile manufactured on TSMC's N6 process. Intel's 18A family also includes 18A-P, claiming up to roughly 9% better performance-per-watt, and 18A-PT for advanced 3DIC integration.

rss · Semianalysis · Sep 26, 13:36

**Background**: Process nodes such as 18A refer to generations of chip manufacturing technology; the '18A' name corresponds to roughly 1.8 nanometers, far smaller than a human hair. Intel 18A is notable because it introduces two key technologies, RibbonFET gate-all-around transistors and PowerVia backside power delivery, which are meant to improve performance and efficiency. Panther Lake is the first client system-on-chip (SoC) family built on 18A, making it a critical test case for Intel's foundry strategy and its plan to build chips for outside customers as well as its own products.

## [US Appeals Court Upholds Pentagon Blacklisting of Anthropic](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-appeals-court-declines-block-pentagons-blacklisting-anthropic-2026-09-25/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

On September 25, the US Court of Appeals for the DC Circuit ruled 2-1 to uphold the Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as a national-security supply-chain risk, barring the company from military contracts. Anthropic said it disagrees with the ruling and is considering asking the full appeals court for an en banc review. The ruling sets an important precedent for how the US government can treat frontier AI labs that place ethical limits on military use of their models, potentially reshaping defense procurement and the leverage labs have in contract negotiations. It also signals that a company's refusal to allow autonomous weapons and mass surveillance applications can itself be framed as a security risk. The majority held that the Pentagon's concerns were reasonable given Anthropic's refusal to permit use of its products for autonomous weapons and mass surveillance, while the dissent indicates the court was split. The case has already produced conflicting outcomes: a federal judge in San Francisco had previously overturned a related listing under a different law and blocked broader government restrictions on Anthropic.

**Background**: Being designated a national-security supply-chain risk effectively blacklists a company from Pentagon contracts, similar to how the US restricts vendors tied to foreign adversaries. Anthropic is a leading frontier AI developer whose usage policies prohibit applying its models to autonomous weapons and mass surveillance, a stance that puts it at odds with defense agencies seeking advanced AI. An en banc review would mean all judges of the DC Circuit rehear the case, rather than just the original three-judge panel. The dispute illustrates the growing tension between AI ethics commitments and government national-security requirements.
