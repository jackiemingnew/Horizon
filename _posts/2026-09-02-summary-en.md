## [Open-Source AI Detectors Fail at 0.5% False-Positive Rate](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1w58erw/most_opensource_ai_detectors_cant_hold_a_05/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In a controlled evaluation, most open-source AI text detectors could not maintain a 0.5% false-positive rate, with several collapsing on humanizer-paraphrased or frontier-model text. The best model reached 93.2% recall on raw AI text but only 41.6% on humanized text, while the old OpenAI RoBERTa detector scored an AUC of 0.313, worse than a coin flip. These results undermine the reliability of free AI detection tools used in schools, hiring, and content moderation, where false accusations can have serious consequences. The consistent bias against non-native English essays also highlights a fairness issue that affects the entire detector class, not just one product. Four of six models could not reach the target 0.5% false-positive rate at any threshold; MAGE assigned scores above 0.9999 to 26% of ordinary human web text. The authors disclosed that one of the six detectors is their own open-weights model, and they published all datasets and methodology so results can be reproduced.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/grumpyp2 · Sep 2, 12:04

**Background**: AI text detectors are machine-learning classifiers that try to distinguish human-written text from text generated by large language models. A false positive occurs when a detector labels human writing as AI-generated; a 0.5% false-positive rate means one in every 200 human texts is wrongly flagged. Humanizer or paraphrasing tools rewrite AI output to make it appear more human, which can evade detection. The findings indicate that open-source detectors trained on older model outputs, such as the GPT-2-based RoBERTa detector, perform poorly on newer generators.
